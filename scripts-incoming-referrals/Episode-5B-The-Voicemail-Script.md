# Episode 5B · The Voicemail

**Logline.** Variation of Episode 5. A Monday message from Barbara at Gentle Dental ("any update on Chloe?") uncovers a small, human slip: Tanya left a voicemail Thursday, was out Friday, and nobody followed up. Nobody is blamed. Amanda opens ToothReferral Analytics and shows the team the slip was never just one patient: the status board lists every referral sitting on "left a voice message", by name and by wait time. They fix it in minutes, Barbara sees the update on the case without calling, and Dr. Vance sees why one saved case pays for the system.

**Setting.** Oakridge Oral Surgery front desk, Monday 8:40 AM, with a split screen to Gentle Dental (the referring general dentist's office). About 2:15, vertical 9:16, 17 clips of 8 seconds. Same cast and looks as Episode 4.

## Cast

- **Tanya**: Front desk coordinator, Oakridge. Left the voicemail Thursday, was out sick Friday. Arc: Caught off guard → relieved → back in control.
- **Greg**: Front desk coordinator, Oakridge. Thought someone had called back. Arc: Puzzled → practical.
- **Amanda**: Office manager, Oakridge. Turns a slip into a system, not a blame session. Arc: Calm, kind, clear.
- **Dr. Vance**: Owner and oral surgeon. Walks in mid-way and asks the owner's questions. Arc: Curious → convinced.
- **Barbara**: Office manager at Gentle Dental, the referring general dentist's office. Arc: Concerned → reassured → loyal.

## The real problems, and how ToothReferral answers each one

| Problem | What ToothReferral shows / does |
|---|---|
| Human error: "I left a voicemail" becomes the last thing anyone did. | Status board: "Left a Voice Message" lists every patient by name with how long they have waited (3 patients, longest 3 days 17 hours). |
| Someone is out, and their follow-ups stop with them. | Every referral lives on the case, not in one person's memory: anyone on the team picks it up. |
| The referring office has to call to find out what happened. | Barbara asks on the case (secure message) and sees the new status herself, no phone tag. |
| Nobody knows how many slipped, only the one that got noticed. | Referral funnel: 3 of 28 never reached first contact, now visible by name. |
| Slow first contact loses patients. | Time to first contact: 3.7 hours on average, tracked against last period. |
| The owner can't see the team's follow-up without asking. | Analytics any time: volume, funnel, clocks, needs attention, staff responsiveness. |
| "Is it worth paying for?" | One saved implant referral can cover the system for a year (potential, typical usage, not a guarantee). |
| "Switching takes forever." | Set up in about 5 minutes. |

## Notes

- Variation of Episode 5: same analytics, different angle. Version A is the owner meeting (where referrals leak); version B starts from one human slip (a voicemail nobody followed up on) and grows into the whole team, the owner and the referring office.
- Follows Vejay's brand-awareness order: the problem as human error (never staff fault), the system that fixes it, the numbers (ROI as "potential"), and the relationship that brings more referrals. Answers what / why / how (set up in about 5 minutes).
- Both sides of the referral: Barbara (general dentist's office) asks on the case and sees the answer herself; Oakridge (specialist) fixes it. Direct communication through secure messages on the case, no phone tag.
- Every number is on the canonical Analytics page: status board "Left a Voice Message" 3 (avg 2d 11h, longest 3d 17h), 28 received (+27%), time to first contact 3.7 h, funnel 28 → 25 (3 never contacted).
- Names follow Vejay's rules: staff are only Barbara, Tanya, Amanda and Greg; Dr. Vance is the only doctor named (series exception). Patient name Chloe Henderson is from the canonical status board.
- Dashboard inserts are rebuilt from the real Analytics page in 3D glass (like Episodes 2 and 4); our own highlights use amber, no red.
- One line per clip, each under about 7 seconds of speech.
