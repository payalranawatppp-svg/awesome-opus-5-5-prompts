# Episode 5 · The Numbers

**Logline.** Dr. Vance thinks his referrals are fine because the schedule is full. Amanda opens ToothReferral Analytics and shows him what a full schedule hides: referrals that leak between "received" and "completed", a loyal referring office that has gone quiet because it never hears back, patients nobody can reach, and insurance checks stuck for days. By the end of the meeting they have fixed every item on the list, and Dr. Vance knows exactly which call to make.

**Setting.** Oakridge Oral Surgery. Monday, 8:05 AM, before the first patient: Dr. Vance's private office (monthly owner meeting), then the front desk. About 2:15, vertical 9:16, 17 clips of 8 seconds. Same cast and looks as Episode 4.

## Cast

- **Dr. Vance**: Owner and oral surgeon, Oakridge Oral Surgery. Smart, busy, runs on instinct and lunch-and-learns. Arc: Comfortable → surprised → uneasy → focused → decisive.
- **Amanda**: Office manager / treatment coordinator. Calm, prepared, lets the numbers do the talking. Arc: Prepared, steady, quietly confident.
- **Tanya**: Front desk coordinator. Fast, organised, owns the follow-ups. Arc: Matter-of-fact, relieved.
- **Greg**: Front desk coordinator. Handles scheduling and insurance. Arc: Easy-going, practical.
- **Lakeside front desk (voice only)**: The referring general dentist's office that has gone quiet. Heard on the phone, never named or shown. Arc: Warm, a little surprised.

## The real problems, and how ToothReferral answers each one

| Problem | What ToothReferral shows / does |
|---|---|
| "The schedule is full, so referrals must be fine." | Volume received + Referral funnel: 28 received, only 9 completed (32% conversion). |
| Referrals leak between steps and nobody sees where. | Funnel shows each drop: 3 never contacted, 5 never scheduled, 11 scheduled with no completion recorded. |
| Patients are seen, but the referring dentist never hears back, so they quietly stop sending. | Needs attention: scheduled appointments past date with no completion note → Mark complete (2). Status board shows whose cases are waiting. |
| You only find out a referrer has gone quiet months later. | Top senders, with a click-through to what each office sent this period. |
| Slow first calls lose patients to the next practice. | Time to first contact: 3.7 hours, tracked every day against the prior period. |
| Patients who never answer sit in limbo. | Ghost referrals (3+ attempts logged): 2, ghost rate 7% → Re-assign, or tell the referring office. |
| Insurance verifications stall and push the appointment back. | Insurance requests outstanding over 48 h: 3 → Send reminder. |
| No idea where referrals actually come from. | Referral sources: Network 19, website request form (Embed widget) 7, Manual 2. |
| Owner reports built by hand in a spreadsheet. | Export PDF, same report every month. |

## Notes

- One line per clip, all under about 6.5 seconds of speech, so each fits one 8-second Flow clip.
- Every number spoken is on the canonical Analytics page and they add up: 28 received; 3 + 5 + 11 = 19 lost between steps; 9 completed; 2 past-date appointments, 2 ghost referrals, 3 insurance requests; 3.7 h to first contact; 7 from the website form.
- Top senders keep the canonical counts (7, 6, 5, 4, 3, 3); names are customised for the series so Gentle Dental (Barbara's office) is the top sender and Lakeside Family Dentistry is the one that has gone quiet.
- Names follow Vejay's rules: staff are only Barbara, Tanya, Amanda and Greg, and Dr. Vance is the only doctor named (series exception). Say "general dentist", never the abbreviation.
- The front desk is never the problem: the leaks come from things nobody could see. Tanya and Greg fix their items in one click.
- Dashboard inserts are rebuilt from the real Analytics page (3D glass, like Episodes 2 and 4). Funnel leak rows are shown in amber in our graphics (no red).
- Monitors and laptops stay angled away in Flow; all screens go in during the edit.
