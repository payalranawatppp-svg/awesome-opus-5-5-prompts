# Script 13 — "Buried in Emails" (two front-desk colleagues) · Google Flow prompts

11 clips × 8 s ≈ 88 s · vertical 9:16 · two women at a dental reception desk.

Follows the client rules: no "GP"; staff names only (Tanya, Amanda); no doctors' names; no red in the frame; the
problem is the email chaos, never the staff (Amanda tells Tanya "that's not on you, that's the system"); the owner
benefit and the ROI are covered; the ending is a free-trial CTA.

## Characters and set (the same block is built into every prompt)

- **TANYA** (at the computer): a woman in her mid-30s with dark brown hair in a low side ponytail over her left
  shoulder. She wears navy short-sleeve V-neck scrubs and a small blank white name badge, with light natural makeup.
- **AMANDA** (the colleague who walks in): a woman in her early 30s with shoulder-length light-brown wavy hair worn
  down. She wears a soft teal scrub top under a light grey cardigan and a small blank white name badge.
- **SET:** the reception of a bright, modern dental office. There is a white and light-oak front desk with a computer
  monitor angled away from the camera (soft glow only, no readable screen), a desk phone, a pen cup, manila folders,
  a small tooth model on the counter, a framed poster of a smiling tooth on the wall, and a doorway behind them
  showing a dental chair and an overhead dental light. There are beige waiting-room chairs and a window with soft
  daylight.
- **VOICES:**
  - Tanya has a warm, mid-to-low female voice in her mid-30s with a natural neutral American accent. She sounds
    tired and frustrated at first, then relieved.
  - Amanda has a bright, friendly, slightly higher female voice in her early 30s with a natural neutral American
    accent. She is calm, reassuring and upbeat.
  - Each keeps the same voice in every clip.

**Edit plan (post):** when Amanda explains a feature (clips 5–9), split the screen with the real ToothReferral
dashboard: the Incoming list, the status board, administrative notes, the message thread with the X-ray, and the
insurance cards. The monitor in the footage never shows text, so the overlays stay clean.

## Flow tips

- Make two still reference images first (Tanya alone at the desk, then both women at the desk). Generate every clip
  from the two-shot still, or from the last frame of the previous clip, so faces, hair, clothes and the room stay the
  same.
- Flow renders on-screen text badly, so keep the monitor angled away. The real dashboard goes in during the edit.
- If both women talk at once, put the speaker first: "Only Tanya speaks…" or "Only Amanda speaks…".
- If a voice drifts between clips, send the clips anyway. I'll match each voice in the edit, re-voicing whole lines,
  never spliced words.
