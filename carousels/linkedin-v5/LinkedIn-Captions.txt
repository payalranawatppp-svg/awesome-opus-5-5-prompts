POST 1: The leak is inside your own office  (upload LinkedIn-Post-1.pdf)

Most specialists think they need more GP referrals. Their biggest leak is inside their own front office.

Look at what happens to the cases you already get.

A voicemail is left on Monday and the person who left it is out sick on Tuesday. Someone clicks "Scheduled" when they meant "Received." A coordinator quits and their follow-ups go with them. In a busy specialty office, that adds up to tens of thousands in lost production every month.

ToothReferral gives your whole team one referral pipeline. Every call, voicemail and status change is logged where everyone can see it, a case can't be marked Scheduled without a real appointment, and the referring GP is updated automatically.

One referral that doesn't leak pays for the system. Setup takes about five minutes.

POST 2: One shared network, not another portal  (upload LinkedIn-Post-2.pdf)

Referral portals fail because they treat your referring doctors like outsiders.

A GP who wants an update on a crown or an implant gets a portal link, an access code, and sometimes a code that expired last week. Many referring offices give up and call your front desk instead, which is the phone tag the portal was meant to end.

ToothReferral puts both offices on the same platform. The GP has their own dashboard and so do you. A case, a message or an X-ray shows up in both, with no codes and no links. Every milestone, from case received to the patient returning for restorative care, reaches the GP in seconds.

GPs rarely leave a specialist over clinical skill. They leave when they stop hearing back. Keep them updated and they keep sending cases, and one extra referral pays for the system. Setup takes about five minutes.

POST 3: Built to survive turnover, built to sell  (upload LinkedIn-Post-3.pdf)

Is your referral pipeline built to transfer, or built on sticky notes?

For many specialty practices, it's the sticky notes. The pipeline lives in one coordinator's head and an inbox only they check. Buyers and DSOs call this key-person risk, and they price it in.

ToothReferral keeps the pipeline in a system instead of a person. A case can't move to Scheduled without an appointment and a note, and every call, note and status change is logged, so a new hire can pick up where the last person stopped.

It pays for itself three ways: one new referral, one referral saved from leaking, or one case saved when someone leaves. Setup takes about five minutes.
