POST 1: The leak is inside your own office  (upload LinkedIn-Post-1.pdf)

Most specialists think they need more referrals from general dentists. Their biggest leak is inside their own front office.

Look at what happens to the cases you already get.

Your front desk juggles phones, patients and paperwork all day, and the tools make it easy for a referral to slip. A voicemail is left on a slammed Monday. On a busy day "Scheduled" gets clicked instead of "Received." A coordinator moves on and their follow-ups go with them. In a busy specialty office, that adds up to tens of thousands in lost production every month.

ToothReferral gives your whole team one referral pipeline. Every call, voicemail and status change is logged where everyone can see it, a case can't be marked Scheduled without a real appointment, and the referring general dentist is updated automatically.

One referral that doesn't leak pays for the system. Setup takes about five minutes.

POST 2: One shared network, not another portal  (upload LinkedIn-Post-2.pdf)

Referral portals fail because they treat your referring doctors like outsiders.

A general dentist who wants an update on a crown or an implant gets a portal link, an access code, and sometimes a code that expired last week. Many referring offices give up and call your front desk instead, which is the phone tag the portal was meant to end.

ToothReferral puts both offices on the same platform. The general dentist has their own dashboard and so do you. A case, a message or a pano shows up in both, with no codes and no links. The referring dentist sees every step, from Received to Consultation Scheduled to Completed, as a live case presentation instead of emails to their office manager.

When the updates stop, general dentists look elsewhere: they try another specialist, or they start doing the procedure themselves. Keep them in the loop and you stay their first call, and one extra referral pays for the system. Setup takes about five minutes.

POST 3: Built to survive turnover, built to sell  (upload LinkedIn-Post-3.pdf)

Is your referral pipeline built to transfer, or built on sticky notes?

For many specialty practices, it's the sticky notes. The pipeline lives in one coordinator's head and an inbox only they check. Buyers and DSOs call this key-person risk, and they price it in.

ToothReferral keeps the pipeline in a system instead of a person. A case can't move to Scheduled without an appointment and a note, and every call, note and status change is logged, so a new hire can pick up where the last person stopped.

It pays for itself three ways. One new referral covers a full year of the software in most cases. One referral saved from leaking covers at least eight months. And one referral won back by great communication is priceless, though it also covers a full year. Setup takes about five minutes.
