Source: the client's notes pasted in chat on 2026-11-02 and `Marketing-tips.pdf` (this folder). Audience: dental
**specialists** (periodontists, endodontists, oral surgeons, orthodontists) and their front office.

## 00. Client review rules (Vejay, 2026-11-04) — apply to EVERY script, video, carousel, caption and still

These override anything below that conflicts (e.g. older lines that say "GP").

1. **Never say or write "GP" / "GPs".** Say **"general dentist(s)"** (or "referring general dentist", "referring
   office"). This applies to dialogue, captions, on-screen text, carousel copy, hashtags/keywords and prompts.
2. **Office names** (demo data): use **Gentle Dental** (was "R Dental") for the referring general dentist office and
   **St. Andrew's Family Dental** (was "D Care Alliance" / "D Alliance") for the specialist office. Oral Surgery
   Associates, Integrity Specialty Dental etc. may stay.
3. **People:** dental assistants, office managers, front desk etc. are only **Barbara, Tanya, Amanda, Greg**.
   **No doctors' names anywhere** (no "Dr. …", no "Lee Davis", "Sarah Chen", "Jonathan Kilbourn" as a doctor). Patient
   names (e.g. Bob Smith) are fine. Roles: Amanda = Office Manager, Greg = Front Desk, Tanya = Front Desk, Barbara =
   Office Manager (vary as needed).
4. **No red** (it reads as blood): alerts, losses, strike-throughs, stamps, flashes and highlights use **amber**

5. **Never condescend to the front desk.** They make most of the buying decisions. Humour aims at the problem (phone

## 01. Client call (Vejay, 2026-11-05, voice memo) — the strategy now; follow it over older sections

**Goal:** a brand-awareness campaign that makes the front desk *like* us ("we're here to help your staff"), shows the
owner the value, and proves ToothReferral's efficiency with real numbers. Carousels and videos must follow ONE
brand-awareness strategy and complement each other. "Go slow and think it through" — understand before writing.

**Brand-awareness structure (in this order):**
1. Present a problem — as *human error*, never as staff fault: someone forgot, someone was out, someone thought they
   called, a referral wasn't processed in time / no first point of contact. You don't fix human error by blaming
   people; you fix it with software that builds a system (an agentic workflow that does the follow-up).
2. Their form vs. our form — research 20+ oral surgery / endodontic / perio websites: why is ToothReferral's
   referral flow better than the forms specialists use today? (A form sends an email; ToothReferral starts a
   tracked, shared case.)
3. Return on investment, with numbers (below). "People have to have a reason to switch."
4. Internal marketing / peer networking is the best way to get patients: show how to market through your own network.

**Story to use (relationship → referrals):** a staff member at a specialist office and one at a general dentist's
office start talking about one patient in ToothReferral; they keep talking; the relationship grows; "when everybody
keeps talking, it's hard to forget you", and sending a referral is easy because nobody has to call. Communication is
the number-one tool for efficiency; even a well-established practice can be 10x better. (He told it with doctor names
— keep to the review rules: staff names Barbara/Tanya/Amanda/Greg, no doctors' names.)

**Show the product doing real work, quickly, by staff:** an assistant writing the consultation note from a template,
signing it, or **routing the note to the doctor** to sign (a routing feature exists — no screenshot yet, ask for one);
the oral surgeon sharing a **case presentation instead of five emails**; all files in the case so nobody ever calls to
ask how to download anything.

**Owner value:** keeps employees on track; the owner can check what's going on any time; it won't break the bank.

**ROI numbers he gave (use as "potential" / "up to", attribute to typical usage, never as guarantees):**
- Cost: about $150–$300 a month (about $1,000–$3,000 a year).
- Saving referrals that would have slipped: potentially about $5,000 a year.
- New referrals from the network: potentially $5,000–$10,000 a year.
- Over 10 years: about $100,000 from referrals (compounding).
- A turnkey, data-backed referral system raises practice value about 5–10% (on a $1M practice: $50,000–$100,000).
- Total: up to about $150,000 back on $10,000–$15,000 spent over 10 years, roughly 10x. (He said "over 150%";
  the correct figure is about 900% net / 10x. Show the math.)
- If owners don't check their referral systems they can lose thousands, and it adds up over time.

**Corrections to earlier scripts:**
- "A status clicked by accident" / "marked Scheduled by mistake" — NOT realistic (Consultation Scheduled etc. are
  unique to ToothReferral). Use "left a voice message, but nobody followed up" (common).
- "A coordinator quits and takes every follow-up with them" — wrong; nobody steals follow-ups. Say: "a coordinator
  leaves, and now nobody knows what still needs to be done."
- No per-incident dollar countdowns ("every disappearing act costs $7,933") — not believable. Say it adds up over time.
- Spliced text-to-speech lines are audible as an accent change (he heard it on "goes straight back to the general
  dentist" in Magic Show). Avoid splicing TTS into a converted take; re-voice whole lines from one source.

**Formats:** he finds every video "podcast format" (one talking head). He wants variety like his examples: split
screens, people moving, B-roll (he suggested Firefly / CapCut). Talking-head is acceptable, but mix in split screens,
dashboard screen recordings and movement.
**Avatar:** keep the female presenter ("I like this girl better"); make it obvious it is a dental office.

## 0. The one rule

Every video and every ad answers three questions, in this order or woven together:

1. **What they're getting** — the software: one shared referral network/dashboard, not a web form or portal.
2. **Why they need it** — the ROI (section 2).
3. **How to implement it** — set up in about **5 minutes**, and it becomes a transferable business asset.

A script that only tours features is not done. Check every script against these three before delivering.

## 1. The three things to convince specialists of

1. **They need a new referral system (brand awareness).** The real leak is not "too few GPs sending cases" — it is
   inside their own front office: missed voicemails, accidental status clicks, staff turnover. That costs tens of
   thousands in lost production every month.
2. **The ROI.** Show it more than one way (pick 1–2 per video, vary across videos):
   - One **new** referral pays for the system.
   - One referral **prevented from leaking** pays for it.
   - One referral that would have been **missed because an employee left** pays for the software for a whole year.
   - GPs refer more to specialists who keep them updated → more repeat cases.
   - Built-for-sale: a transferable pipeline raises practice value at exit or partner buyout.
   Don't invent prices or dollar figures beyond "tens of thousands a month" and "pays for itself with one referral";
   if a number is needed, ask the user.
3. **It's easy.** Integrates in **5 minutes**, and builds a transferable data asset that keeps earning every day.

## 2. Core lines (client wording, usable nearly verbatim)

- "Most dental specialists think their referral problem is getting more GPs to send cases. In reality, their biggest
  leak is inside their own front office — missed voicemails, accidental status clicks, and staff turnover."
- "Toothreferral isn't another static web form. It's an agentic referral pipeline that eliminates human error,
  coordinates follow-ups across your entire team, and keeps your referring doctors updated in real time."
- "Even if your entire front desk turns over tomorrow, your referral revenue doesn't skip a beat."
- "Portals fail because they treat your referring doctors like outsiders who have to prove their identity with access
  codes every time they want an update."
- "When an office sends you a case, it syncs directly between both of your dashboards. No access codes, no portal
  links, no friction."
- "A generic web form belongs to your website developer. Toothreferral is a pipeline that transfers to a buyer."
- "GPs don't leave specialists over clinical skill — they leave over communication black holes."
- "Crystal-clear communication is your highest-margin marketing strategy."
- "That web form is just a digital fax machine."

## 3. Positioning: one shared network, not a portal

- **Symmetrical B2B:** both the GP and the specialist log into their own dashboard on the same platform. A specialist's
  message instantly updates the GP's patient feed — no codes, links or redirects. (Contrast: portals/encrypted emails
  make the GP an outsider.)
- **Shared workspace:** "like Slack or Teams, built only for dental referrals." Coordinators, assistants and doctors on
  both sides see treatment status, radiographs, clinical questions and restorative schedules.
- **Partnership, not vendor vs client:** the specialist isn't forcing a portal on the GP; both offices get organised,
  trackable pipelines with no phone tag, faxes or unmonitored inboxes.

## 4. The three pillars (Marketing-tips.pdf)

| Pillar | Problem | ToothReferral answer |
|---|---|---|
| Build for sale (equity) | Practices run on memory and sticky notes sell at a discount ("key-person risk"); coordinator leaves → pipeline stalls | Codified, self-running, auditable pipeline that transfers to a buyer and supports a higher valuation |
| Agentic workflows (error + turnover) | A misclick marks a case "Scheduled"; a voicemail is left, then the staffer is out sick and the lead goes cold | Rule-enforced status changes (no "Scheduled" without calendar data and notes); every outreach/voicemail visible to all staff; stalled cases re-queued automatically, 24/7 |
| Closed loop in seconds | GPs leave over silence; static forms give no SEO value | Milestones push to the GP automatically (Received → Outreach Made → Consult Scheduled → Post-Op Complete → Returned for Restorative); supports the practice's own domain authority; GPs send more to specialists who return patients fast |

Objection answers to borrow: "We already have a form" → digital fax machine, nobody guarantees it gets booked.
"Not selling for 10 years" → built-for-sale just means it doesn't break when people leave; profits now.
"How does it make me money?" → stops leakage, and closes the GP loop so they keep referring.

## 5. How to use this in scripts

- Hook (from `hook-reference`) aimed at the **front-office leak** or **turnover**, not "get more referrals".
- Middle: what they get (shared dashboard, shown on the real recording), and at least one ROI line from section 1.2.
- Before the CTA: the "5 minutes to set up" line.
- CTA: soft, one line ("check out Tooth Referral").
- Only show on screen what the dashboard recording actually shows (incoming list, statuses, chat with @mentions, X-rays,
  insurance cards, activity log, status update + success toast, PDF download). For agentic features not in the
  recording (rule-enforced statuses, auto re-queue, milestone pushes), say them in dialogue and use motion graphics,
  not fake UI that looks like the real product.
